## Training: how it was done

Can a small open model learn this job from the environment alone? Qwen3-4B, with thinking off and LoRA adapters on
one GPU at a time [@qwen3; @hu2021lora], trains on battery-days of 2014 to 2024 and is scored on days it never saw.
Every registered run committed its plan, its schedule and the bars it had to clear before its first step; the
pre-registrations are in [`docs/prereg/`](https://github.com/earakely-scale/agentenv-gridsim-plugin/tree/main/docs/prereg).

### One prompt, one completion, the environment's own grader

Training runs on **path B**: one prompt and one completion per battery-day, graded in-process, with no environment
episodes inside the training loop. The prompt is exactly what the environment's one-call harness sends, so the trained
model plays the environment as it trained. The completion must end in a `submit_schedule` call. The reward is
`gridsim_core.trl.reward_fn`, the same grader the environment's verifier runs, which a test checks to 1e-6; it grades a
completion in under a millisecond, with no format credit.

```python
from datasets import load_dataset
from trl import GRPOConfig, GRPOTrainer
from gridsim_core.trl import reward_fn

train = load_dataset("earakely-scale/GridSimEnv-AgentEnv", "v1_tasks",
                     split="train")
train = train.filter(lambda r: r["tier"] == "foresight")
trainer = GRPOTrainer(model="Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B", reward_funcs=reward_fn,
                      train_dataset=train,
                      args=GRPOConfig(output_dir="gridsim-grpo"))
trainer.train()
```

### Step 1: learn from the solver

At the foresight tier the dynamic program gives the optimal schedule of every training day for free, so the first
step is supervised fine-tuning on solver labels. What the labels explain matters more than how many there are. In the
first runs, on Qwen3-1.7B, a rationale that only listed the trades taught the format, but the plans broke the rules:
they bought into full batteries and ended the day short. A rationale that tracks the state of charge hour by hour and
states the solver's rule, *a buy at b and a sell at s pay if s × η > b*, works. Ten thousand such examples (**SFT-10k**) took the 4B from
0.06 to 0.841 on 200 held-out foresight days of 2025, with no RL. A pre-registered check on 500 unseen days of 2014 to
2024 then confirmed it: 0.880 [0.858, 0.900] against the greedy rule's 0.751 [0.724, 0.777].

Here is what the trained 4B (run 3, below) writes before its call on a held-out day, 16 July 2025 at the Houston hub:

```text
Starts at 3/6. Must end at >= 3/6; cycle cap 12 units sold.
Rule: a buy at b and a sell at s pay if s x 0.9 > b. Pairs: sell HE01 -> buy back HE09-HE10 x3: $27 x 0.9 = $24 > $17;
buy HE09-HE10 -> sell HE20-HE21 x3: $59 x 0.9 = $53 > $17; sell HE20-HE21 -> buy back HE24 x3: $74 x 0.9 = $67 > $36.
Plan: sell HE01 (3 units, $27) -> 0/6; buy HE09-HE10 (6 units, $17) -> 6/6; sell HE20-HE21 (6 units, $59-$74) -> 0/6;
buy HE24 (3 units, $36) -> 3/6.
End check: ends at 3/6 vs >= 3/6 required, ok; sold 9 of 12 cap.
```

The warm start is not optional. GRPO from the untrained 1.7B never produced a valid schedule, so every group of
rollouts scored the same 0 and there was no gradient at all.

### Step 2: GRPO stalls on a confident policy

GRPO [@shao2024deepseekmath] samples a group of completions for each prompt and gives every token of a completion the
same advantage, its reward relative to the group's:

$$
\hat{A}_i = \frac{R_i - \operatorname{mean}_j R_j}{\operatorname{std}_j R_j}, \qquad j = 1, \dots, G
$$

The first two registered runs used TRL's `GRPOTrainer` with colocated vLLM rollouts, eight completions per prompt and
the Dr. GRPO loss [@trl; @kwon2023vllm; @liu2025drgrpo].

- **Run 1**, from a weaker warm start (1,500 solver examples), gained +0.138 [+0.092, +0.185] in 330 steps, nearly all
  of it in the first hundred. Then the policy's entropy collapsed, and up to 45% of the groups had no reward spread.
- **Run 2**, from SFT-10k, moved −0.015 [−0.061, +0.030] in 310 steps, even with learnability-weighted sampling and
  zero-spread groups resampled away. The warm start was nearly deterministic: on about half of the days all eight
  rollouts wrote the same schedule, got the same reward and gave the model nothing to learn.

### Step 3: exact advantages from the dynamic program

A battery-day has a property most RL tasks lack: the optimal value of every state is computable. The dynamic program
that grades a schedule also gives V\*(s), the best profit still achievable from any state, and Q\*(s, a), the best
profit after taking action *a* there, where the state *s* is the hour, the charge and the units sold so far
[@bellman1957]. So each hour of a completion can be credited exactly, along the path the completion itself took:

$$
A_t = Q^{*}(s_t, a_t) - V^{*}(s_t), \qquad \sum_{t=1}^{24} A_t = R - 1
$$

In reward units the hours sum to the completion's whole shortfall from the optimum. Every A<sub>t</sub> is zero on an
optimal schedule and never positive, and an hour that breaks a rule carries the value of the rest of the day it
throws away. No group, no baseline and no critic is needed, and a single rollout carries signal even when every
rollout agrees.

**Run 3** replaced GRPO's group advantage with this one. The credit lands on the tokens of the 24 integers: the sign
token of each hour gets the advantage of the best action of that sign, its digit token the rest, and every other
token gets R − 1. The update is a PPO-clipped policy gradient with a KL anchor to SFT-10k [@schulman2017ppo], in a
custom PyTorch and PEFT loop with vLLM rollouts, not TRL's trainer: 300 steps of 32 training days each.

- **Held-out reward rose +0.041 [+0.011, +0.072]**, to 0.885, and +0.039 [+0.022, +0.057] on 500 unseen days of 2014

- **The gain came from fewer broken schedules.** Feasible schedules went from 83.0% to 89.5% and overfills from 10.5%

- **It is a weak and noisy trend from one seed:** the slope's lower bound is only just above zero, and the curve dips
  after step 175 before ending high.

### Blind: forecast, then plan

At the blind tier the solver's labels are the wrong target, because they were computed from prices the agent cannot
see. **Run 4** split the job in two. It continued from run 3 with supervised fine-tuning on 12,500 rows in which the
completion first writes a 24-hour forecast, made by a tuned forecaster (an ensemble of ridge, LightGBM and an MLP
fitted on the training years), and then the exact plan on that forecast. RL then applied the same exact advantages to
the plan, computed on the model's own written forecast; the forecast tokens got no credit and stayed anchored to the
fine-tuned model.

- **Its forecasts are good.** They miss by \$9.79/MWh over all hours of 2025, against the tuned forecaster's \$9.53 and
  yesterday's prices' \$12.05.
- **Planned exactly, they would tie Sol:** 0.907 against Sol's 0.903 on 2025 (+0.004 [−0.012, +0.020], reported, not
  registered).
- **Its own plans lose 0.079.** Only 46% of its schedules are the exact plan on the forecast it wrote just above them,
  and 10% break a rule. The registered claim, that the trained 4B is no worse than Sol, is not met:
  −0.075 [−0.101, −0.050] on 2025 and −0.072 [−0.090, −0.050] on the newest days. RL on the plan added little here:
  +0.006 [−0.015, +0.026] on 2025 and +0.023 [+0.007, +0.038] on the newest days.

<Note variant="neutral" title="Not run yet">
Two consequences follow, neither tested. A planning tool in the loop, the exact DP for both models, would turn the blind
comparison into a forecasting contest the 4B can enter at a tuned forecaster's quality. And a DAgger-style term
toward the solver's action on the model's own forecast, which the trainer already computes, might close the planning
gap without a tool [@ross2011dagger].
</Note>

### What it cost

About \$156 of GPU in all, from the first 1.7B arm to run 4, on single NVIDIA L4, L40S and A10G GPUs, with no API
calls in training. Run 3 cost about \$31 and run 4 about \$40. Against renting the same blind score from GPT-6 Luna at
high effort, run 4's bill is repaid by its inference savings after about 26,000 battery-days, run 3's after about
11,000. The adapters are not published.
