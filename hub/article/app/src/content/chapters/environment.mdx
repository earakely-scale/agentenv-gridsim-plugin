## What is in GridSimEnv

GridSimEnv puts an agent on the trading desk of a real battery on the Texas grid. It is built from the records of
ERCOT, which runs that grid: its real prices, forecasts, notices and grid conditions, each shown to the agent only from
the minute it was posted. The agent runs the battery for three days at a time while a real weather front crosses Texas.
It works through the desk's own tools: it reads prices, forecasts and the battery's status, asks the `optimize` planner
for a plan, offers the battery's energy and reserves into ERCOT's day-ahead and real-time markets, and keeps the current
operating plan it owes ERCOT up to date. Each window settles in dollars on the battery's ERCOT statement and is scored
by hindsight capture C: the share of the hindsight-optimal profit that the agent captured. The benchmark plays
{F.env.windows} windows, {F.env.fronts} fronts at each of {F.env.sites} real batteries, shown here as S01 to S04.

Every run plays out on a 3D battery site in its part of Texas, in the real light and weather of those days. The awards
and dispatch the market gives the agent's offers turn into the battery charging and discharging, and the clock stops
while the agent works and runs on between its turns.

### Before and after: the real operator and the agents

Every window also happened for real. The battery's own operator traded those same three days, and ERCOT publishes what
each battery did 60 days later: its day-ahead awards, its output and its charge every five minutes
[@ercot_np3966; @ercot_np3965]. That gives each window a before and an after. Here are the {F.duel.n} windows where the
agents most clearly beat the operator, all on the {F.duel.span} {F.duel.kind}, after every model's training
cutoff[^pick]. Each shows the operator beside the run that made the most there, {F.duel.best}'s.

Most of each gap is day-ahead trading. Through most of this heat wave the day-ahead price ran above real time, and
{F.duel.best} sold far more energy a day ahead than the battery could deliver, then bought it back in real time,
cheaper; other models traded the same way on a smaller scale. ERCOT's day-ahead market allows such positions because it
ignores a battery's charge; its credit screening, not modelled here, would limit them. They do not always pay: on the
next front, {F.duel.next.span}, real-time prices spiked to about {F.duel.next.rt} a MWh, and the operator came out ahead
of at least {F.duel.next.behind} of the models at each of its {F.duel.next.sites} batteries.
