## The environment

An episode is one **battery-day**: one battery at one settlement point on one date. The agent gets a situation with the
battery's power, energy, efficiency and limits, its starting charge and the charge it must end with, and the prices.
It answers with one call, `submit_schedule`, carrying 24 integers, the first for the hour ending 1 am (HE01) and the
last for the hour ending midnight (HE24). A positive number sells that many units of energy in the hour, a negative one
buys, zero idles.

The battery moves energy in whole units and holds between zero and a fixed number of them. Five rules govern a
schedule:

1. Each hour, the action stays within the battery's power: it can buy at most `k_ch` units and sell at most `k_dis`.
2. The state of charge stays between empty and full after every hour.
3. The units sold over the day stay within a cycle cap.
4. At the first hour that breaks rule 1, 2 or 3, that hour and every later hour execute as idle. The hours before still
   settle.
5. After the last hour the battery must hold at least its end-of-day obligation. Each missing unit is bought back at
   the day's highest price, or \$50/MWh if that is higher.

Selling a unit delivers its energy; buying one draws a little more, because the battery's round-trip losses fall on
the buy side. Each unit sold also costs a flat degradation charge, from \$0 to \$8 per MWh. A negative price pays the
battery to buy. The batteries are archetypes, not real sites: 1, 2 and 4-hour designs from 7.5 to 225 MW, sized from
the ERCOT fleet in EIA-860.

### Two tiers

The same battery-day plays at two tiers, which differ in what the agent knows.

| | Foresight | Blind |
|---|---|---|
| The agent sees | the day's 24 prices | the prices of the day before and of the 7 days before, at the same point |
| It tests | planning: turning known prices into the best feasible schedule | forecasting and planning, as a real desk bids the day before |
| Scored against | the exact optimum on the day's prices | the better of two forecast-driven references (below) |
| Solved? | GPT-6.1 Sol scores 0.9999 on 500 held-out days | no model reaches hindsight |

At the foresight tier the problem has a known answer, which is what makes it a clean place to measure and to train
planning. At the blind tier the agent has to guess the day from its history, so even a perfect planner leaves money on
the table.

### The grade

A schedule's profit **P** settles under the rules above at the day's real prices. It is scored against the profit of
doing nothing, **P**<sub>idle</sub>, and an anchor, **P**<sub>anchor</sub>:

$$
R = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(0.2 + 0.8\,\frac{P - P_{\text{idle}}}{P_{\text{anchor}} - P_{\text{idle}}},\ 0,\ 1\right)
$$

- Doing nothing scores 0.2, the anchor 1.0, and a reply with no valid schedule 0. There is no format credit and no
  judge: the same function grades in the environment, in its verifier and in training.
- **At the foresight tier the anchor is the optimum**, P<sub>opt</sub>, which the dynamic program finds over every
  state of charge, every count of units sold and every action.
- **At the blind tier the anchor is max(persistence-DP, ridge-DP)**: the exact optimum on yesterday's prices, or on a
  ridge regression's forecast of the day, whichever earned more on the actual day. Because it picks the better of two
  in hindsight, it is an oracle no desk could run.
- **The blind results in this article report hindsight capture**, written *r*<sub>opt</sub>: the same formula with
  the optimum as the anchor, so idle is 0.2 and the hindsight optimum 1.0 on one scale for every model.

### How a schedule fails

Because the optimum and the rules are exact, every mistake can be named. Each graded schedule gets one kind:

- **Overfill:** a buy that would take the charge above full.
- **Sell before charge:** a sale from an empty battery.
- **Rate, cycle cap:** an hour beyond the battery's power, or a sale beyond the day's cycle cap.
- **End short:** a feasible day that ends below the obligation, so the shortfall is bought back at the peak.
- **All idle:** 24 zeros, which scores exactly 0.2.
- **No valid schedule:** no parseable call, or a reply cut off while thinking, which scores 0.
