## How the models did

Every model in this chapter plays the same harness: the environment's one-call prompt, the battery and the prices (or,
blind, the days before) in, one `submit_schedule` call out, no practice checks. At the blind tier one extra paragraph
lets the model write a 24-hour forecast before it plans. Training uses exactly this prompt, so trained and frontier
models meet on equal terms. The models are GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna through their API, Beam-1.0, an open-weights
model served through the Hugging Face router, and Qwen3-4B before and after training on one GPU [@qwen3]. Reasoning
models think as long as their effort setting allows.

- **Foresight is nearly solved at the top.** With the day's prices in view, GPT-6.1 Sol finds the optimum on almost
  every day: 1.000 on both foresight packs here, and 0.9999 on 500 held-out days with the environment's full
  three-tool harness on the [v1 board](https://github.com/earakely-scale/agentenv-gridsim-plugin/blob/main/results/board.md).
- **Blind is where models separate.** Seeing only the days before, Sol captures 0.913 of hindsight on ERCOT's newest
  days and 0.903 on 2025. A rule with no language model, the exact optimum on yesterday's prices (persistence-DP),
  captures 0.871 and 0.882. On 2025 only Sol scores above it; on the newest days, Sol and Beam at its maximum effort
  do.
- **The untrained 4B cannot play.** Without training, Qwen3-4B scores 0.06 at foresight, mostly because its replies do
  not parse, and 0.17 blind, below doing nothing, because it either idles all day or sells before it has charged.
- **Training changes that.** The trained 4B scores 0.885 and 0.855 at foresight and 0.828 and 0.841 blind. It is
  level with GPT-6 Luna at high effort on both blind packs and on 2025's foresight days, behind it on the newest
  foresight days, and behind Sol everywhere.

### Score against cost

A battery-day costs very different amounts to bid. Sol thinks for about 1,200 output tokens on a blind day, Luna at
high effort for 3,700, Beam at its maximum effort for 26,800. The 4B writes about 430 tokens, on one GPU.

- **The trained 4B is the cheapest point on the frontier above the untrained one.** It is level with GPT-6 Luna at high
  effort, +0.005 [−0.036, +0.044], at a quarter of the cost, and ahead of Luna at low effort, its matched cost, by
  +0.151 [+0.085, +0.197].
- **GPT-6.1 Sol captures 0.072 more** [+0.050, +0.090] for 33 times the 4B's cost per battery-day.
- **Beam-1.0 is off the frontier at every effort.** Its score rises with effort and so does its cost. At its maximum
  effort it captures 0.890, ahead of the trained 4B (+0.049 [+0.007, +0.101]) and level with Sol within the interval,
  for $0.0330 a battery-day: twice Sol's cost, and Sol captures more.
- The 4B, Luna at high and low effort and Sol are the pre-registered rows of the cost-matched contest; Luna at medium
  effort and Beam are descriptive runs under the same harness.

### How models fail

The exact grade makes every failure countable. The frontier models almost always keep the rules; when they fail,
they fail to submit at all, a reply cut off while thinking or with no parseable call. The trained 4B is the reverse:
it always submits, and on some days its schedule breaks a rule.

The leaderboard is built from records. Each model's per-task schedules are committed under `results/`, re-graded by
the same code as the environment, and aggregated by `scripts/hub_results.py`; adding a model means adding its records
and one line to that script, and rerunning the build.
