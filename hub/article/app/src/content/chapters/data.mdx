## The data

The prices are ERCOT's *Historical DAM Load Zone and Hub Prices* (report NP4-180-ER), the yearly files for 2014 to
2026: the hourly day-ahead settlement point price at the 7 trading hubs (Houston, North, Panhandle from April 2019,
South, West, and the hub and bus averages) and the 8 load zones (Austin Energy, CPS Energy, Houston, LCRA, North,
Rayburn, South, West) [@ercot_np4180]. The batteries are sized from Form EIA-860 for 2025: the 204 operable storage
units of ERCOT's balancing authority, 13,115 MW, with a mean duration of 1.645 hours weighted by power [@eia860].

### From a year of prices to a battery-day

1. **Clean.** Days of the daylight-saving changes are dropped. 67,810 point-days remain.
2. **De-duplicate.** Within a date, a point whose 24 prices match one already kept (correlation above 0.999 and no
   hour more than \$1 apart) is dropped: mostly load zones that track their hub, and the two averages.
3. **Split by date.** 2025 and later never reach training (the timeline above).
4. **Give each point-day a battery.** A stable hash picks a duration class (1 hour 40%, 2 hours 45%, 4 hours 15%)
   and one of 26 designs in it: 7.5 to 225 MW, 80% to 90% round-trip efficiency, \$0 to \$8/MWh of wear, 1 to 2
   cycles a day, varied start and end charge, up to 16 charge levels. The 4-hour class is over-weighted on purpose,
   to give long-duration days. They are archetypes, not real sites.
5. **Filter.** A battery-day whose optimum beats idle by less than \$22 per MW of power is dropped as low-spread, and
   80% of the days a simple greedy rule already solves are dropped, so the packs are not padded with easy days.
6. **Sample.** Train takes 20,000 of the 27,688 surviving candidates; the held-out packs are drawn round-robin over
   point, battery class and month.

Every battery-day in the [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/earakely-scale/GridSimEnv-AgentEnv) carries the
exact prompt the agent sees, its battery, the prices, the blind inputs, the exact optimum and the baselines' schedules.

### What is real and what is simplified

The prices are real and so is the settlement: each schedule is paid what ERCOT's day-ahead market actually cleared at
that point and hour. The simplifications are stated where every result is reported: the battery is a price taker
that moves no price; its day-ahead award is its physical schedule; steps are hourly; losses fall on the buy side;
wear is linear; and there are no reserves, no real-time prices and no congestion.
