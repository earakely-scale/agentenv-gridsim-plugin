## Why it matters

ERCOT's battery fleet has grown fast, and what each megawatt earns has fallen. As more batteries compete to sell
reserves, those prices drop, and ERCOT's market monitor reports batteries shifting their business to energy arbitrage:
buying and selling energy at the right hours [@imm2026som; @modo2026ercot; @ercot2026factsheet].

That is the decision GridSimEnv grades. It isolates one piece of a real battery's revenue: the day-ahead schedule,
settled at the prices the market posted. A real desk also sells reserves and trades in real time, which later versions
of the environment add. The day-ahead decision has two properties that make it unusually good for training agents:
its inputs are public and complete, and the best possible decision for any past day can be computed exactly
[@bellman1957]. Few real jobs let you grade an agent against the provable optimum, in dollars, on twelve years of real
days.
