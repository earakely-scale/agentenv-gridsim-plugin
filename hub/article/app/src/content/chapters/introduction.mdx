## The desk

Most RL environments ask an agent for one answer. A grid battery is not run that way. Behind it sits a trading desk
that bids the next day's market in the morning, learns its awards after lunch, offers its real-time curves in the
evening and then watches the operating day unfold five minutes at a time, with ERCOT's notices, the weather and its
own plant to react to. **GridSimEnv Desk** turns that job into an environment, on the Texas grid's real records.

- **A real battery.** The agent is the battery trader at a qualified scheduling entity (QSE), the registered party
  that trades in ERCOT's markets for one real energy storage resource (ESR) at its own resource node. A desk day runs
  from 06:00 the day before the operating day to midnight on it. The batteries are real; here they appear only as
  cards, like Battery S02, Houston, 100 MW / 206 MWh.
- **ERCOT's own objects.** The agent never moves a megawatt itself. It submits energy bid/offer curves into the
  day-ahead market (DAM) before its 10:00 close, real-time curves from 18:00 the day before, and its Current
  Operating Plan (COP), all checked against ERCOT's rules [@ercot_protocols], under the market design in force since
  real-time co-optimization went live in December 2025 [@ercot2025rtcb]. The market's clearing decides what the
  battery does.
- **Information as it was published.** Each price, each forecast vintage, each ERCOT notice, NWS alert and outage
  becomes visible at the minute it was posted, never earlier.
- **Graded in dollars.** P is the battery's settled ERCOT statement. The headline, C, is the share of the hindsight
  optimum's profit over idle that the desk captures, and the battery's real operator, through its own disclosed bids,
  is the yardstick.
