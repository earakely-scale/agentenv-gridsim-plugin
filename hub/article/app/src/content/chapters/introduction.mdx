## A battery desk on the Texas grid

Every morning, Texas's grid operator, ERCOT, runs a day-ahead market. By 10 am, sellers and buyers send offers for each
hour of the next day, and the market posts an hourly price at every hub and load zone [@ercot2025dam]. A grid battery makes money by
buying power when it is cheap, at night or at a sunny midday when solar pushes prices toward zero and sometimes below,
and selling it back into the evening peak. Somebody has to decide, the day before, when.

GridSimEnv turns that decision into an RL environment. In v1, **The Battery Desk**, an agent runs one battery at one of
ERCOT's 7 trading hubs or 8 load zones for one day. It reads the battery and the day's 24 hourly prices, or only the
days before, and submits 24 integers: how many units of energy to buy or sell in each hour. The schedule settles at
that day's real prices and is graded exactly.

Three things make it a good environment to train on.

- **Real records.** The prices are ERCOT's own, every hourly day-ahead price at 15 settlement points from 2014 to
  2026, and the batteries are sized from the Texas fleet in the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Form EIA-860.
  There are 21,250 battery-days, with 2025 held out.
- **An exact optimum.** A dynamic program finds the best possible schedule for any day in under a millisecond. Every
  schedule gets an exact grade with no judge, and, as the training chapter shows, every hour of every schedule gets
  exact credit.
- **Dollars.** The grade is a share of real money. Every result converts to dollars per megawatt of battery a year, at
  the prices the market actually cleared.
