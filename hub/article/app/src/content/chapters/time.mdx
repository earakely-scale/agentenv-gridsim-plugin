## How time works

A desk's day has a clock that does not wait. The desk's clock is AgentEnv's virtual clock, and it stands still while
the agent works. There is no `advance` tool: to wait, the agent ends its turn. A post-turn hook then asks the
environment for its next wake-up and the gateway for its next time trigger, moves the clock to the earlier of the two,
and the environment runs the market forward to that minute: the DAM clears at the node's real day-ahead price, each
5-minute dispatch run (SCED) clears the curve in force at the node's real price within the battery's limits
[@ercot_np6788], and each 15-minute interval settles. Then the agent wakes, with the reasons.

- **Wake-ups are the market's.** The start; the DAM results at the minute ERCOT posted them; the adjustment period
  at 18:00 the day before; shift checkpoints at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00; every ERCOT notice, NWS warning or
  watch, forced outage and, from v3, reserve deployment at its real minute; and any reminder the agent set with
  `set_reminder`.
- **Information as published.** A 5-minute price is visible a minute after its interval and a 15-minute settlement
  price two minutes after; DAM prices, forecast vintages and notices at their posting time. The real operator's bids
  stay hidden until ERCOT discloses them, 60 days later.
- **The turn budget is the scarce resource.** A desk that wakes for everything runs out of turns before the evening
  peak. The benchmark plays the desk through AgentEnv's gateway and the development runs play it in process; the
  desk's tests check that the two paths give byte-identical tool outputs and wake-ups.
